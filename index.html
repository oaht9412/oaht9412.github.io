🐳 DockerUpdates - Keep Your Containers Always Fresh

✨ What DockerUpdates Does For You

Think of DockerUpdates as a control panel for your containers. A container is a small, self-contained piece of software — the way most self-hosted apps are now packaged. DockerUpdates keeps an eye on all of them at once and gives you buttons for the things you actually want to do.

🔄 Manage

See every container in one list with its current state — running, stopped or unhealthy. Start, stop and restart with a single click.

⬆️ Update

Install newer versions of an app without hunting for the right commands. DockerUpdates handles the replacement for you.

📊 Monitor

Watch health, status and activity so problems are noticed before they turn into a broken evening.

⏰ Scheduled Auto-Updates With a Cooldown

Turning on automatic updates does not mean losing control. You decide when checks happen, and a built-in cooldown stops the dashboard from updating the same container over and over in a short window. That protection matters: it prevents endless restart loops and gives you breathing room to notice if something goes wrong.

  • Choose a schedule that fits your routine — nightly is the most common choice.
  • Set a cooldown period so a container is not touched again immediately after an update.
  • Keep automatic updates switched off for apps you prefer to update by hand, and on for the rest.
  • Review the result of every automatic run afterwards in the update history.

🔔 Notifications Where You Already Are

You should not have to stare at a dashboard to know what happened. DockerUpdates can send messages to the places you already check, so a failed update is never a silent surprise.

  • Discord — a message in your personal server or a dedicated channel.
  • Telegram — a chat message straight to your phone or a group.
  • ntfy — a lightweight push notification to your device, ideal for home labs.

Each integration is optional. Turn on one, turn on all three, or none at all — the dashboard works perfectly well without any of them.

🩺 Health Alerts and Live Logs

Containers can keep running while quietly misbehaving. Health alerts watch for that and tell you when something stops responding or reports a problem. When you need detail, open the live logs and interactive console: scrolling text straight from the container appears in your browser, and you can type into it the same way you would in a terminal window — without ever opening a terminal window.

🕘 Update History and the Container Editor

Every update is recorded, so you can answer the question that always comes up: “what changed, and when?” The history view lists what was updated, when it happened and whether it succeeded.

The container editor lets you adjust settings through a normal form instead of editing configuration text. Change a port, a folder, an environment value or a restart rule, and save. For anyone who has ever broken a working setup with one wrong character in a configuration file, this is the feature that makes the whole tool worthwhile.

🛡️ Is It Safe to Use?

DockerUpdates is self-hosted, which means it runs on your own machine and your information stays with you. There is no account to create and no cloud service in the middle. Because the dashboard can control containers, treat access to it like access to the machine itself: keep it on your own network, or protect it properly if you ever make it reachable from outside.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to be a programmer?

No. The whole point of the dashboard is that everyday tasks are clicks instead of typed commands. If you can use a website, you can use DockerUpdates.

Will it update things without asking?

Only if you enable automatic updates. Otherwise every change happens when you click, and the cooldown settings protect you even in automatic mode.

Can I try it without turning on notifications?

Yes. Notifications are entirely optional and can be added later whenever you want them.
